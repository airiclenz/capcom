---
title: "ADR 0005 amendment of 2026-11-22: the lock record also names the holder's harness process, to recognise the holder's own writes; the token still decides acquire, resume, touch and release"
---

Amended 2026-11-22: the lock record also names the holder's harness process, to recognise the
holder's own writes, while the Lock token still decides acquire, resume, touch and release. The
decision holds that an agent session, not a process, holds the lock: an orchestrator runs every
command in a fresh shell, so the pid of capcom or of its shell would look gone the moment acquire
returned. That still stands.

**Which process.** The record's `process` is the nearest ancestor of capcom that is not a shell or a
wrapper command, the harness the session runs in, which outlives each shell and is an ancestor of
every sub-agent it starts. It is stored as PID and start stamp, so a reused PID never matches, and
`lock acquire` and `capcom start` write it when they take or resume the lock.

**What it is used for.** Only to recognise the holder: a caller whose process ancestry holds it
writes without a lock warning and renews the heartbeat (ADR 0017 amendment of 2026-11-22), and runs
touch, release and check without `--token` (ADR 0016 amendment of 2026-11-22). It never decides
whether a lock is live: a lock is live or stale by its heartbeat alone, and a holder process that
has exited leaves the lock held until it is resumed, goes stale and is taken over, or is broken.

**The token still decides.** Acquire, resume, touch and release act through the Lock token, given
with `--token` or kept in the token file; the holder process only lets capcom pick the kept token
for the caller. A record without a `process`, written by an older capcom or by an acquire whose
process ancestry could not be read, works through the token alone, and an older capcom's touch or
resume drops the field.
